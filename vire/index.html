The shape is the tuning.

Vire holds 400 strings between two bronze rings and has no fixed form. Turn a ring and every string changes length at once.

The instrument takes a new shape and a new tuning in the same movement. Scroll to turn the rings.

Run the pointer across the strings
Position I

Unison

Rings level, no twist. Every string runs straight up, 2.40 m long.

The bronze course sounds G3 and the steel course the G below it. Wherever you touch the instrument, it answers with one note.

Position II

Waist

Turn the upper ring 110°. No string bends, yet the wall of strings draws in.

Each string now has further to travel, 2.91 m from ring to ring. Longer strings sound lower, and the whole instrument drops a tone to F.

Position III

Crossing

At a half turn all 200 bronze strings pass through a single point.

The steel course stops 28° short and closes a collar around it. This is as long as the strings get, 3.12 m, and as low as Vire plays: D, a fourth below unison.

Position IV

Lean

Tilt the upper ring 26° and slide it off the axis.

Now no two neighbours are the same length. A scale runs around the rim, rising on one side and falling on the other. Sweep the strings to hear it.

Position V

Rose

Bring the rings together and the instrument lies flat as a wheel.

The strings become chords of one circle, 1.88 m each, and leave a clear eye in the middle. It is the shortest and highest position: C, a fourth above unison.

The instrument

Two rings, nothing else.

Players stand inside or beside it. One turns the rings while the others play.
